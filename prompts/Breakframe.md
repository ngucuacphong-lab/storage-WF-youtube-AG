# Break Frame Rules

## Purpose
Break Frames interrupt the normal storytelling rhythm to regain viewer attention and increase retention.
A Break Frame should create a short but powerful interruption before naturally returning to the main story.

---

## Break Frame Types

### Type A — Question
Present an engaging, thought-provoking question related to the story.
The question should make viewers pause and think before the story continues.

Content rules:
- Write a full, specific question (NOT just a letter or label).
- The question must relate directly to the surrounding story.
- Use curiosity-driven language.

Examples:
- "Did you know ancient humans could run for 8 hours straight without stopping?"
- "Can dogs actually understand grammar — or just tone?"
- "What if your pet was smarter than your phone?"

### Type B — Interesting Fact
Present a short, memorable, and surprising fact or statistic related to the topic.

Content rules:
- Write a complete factual sentence with a real number or specific detail.
- The fact should make viewers say "wow" or "I didn't know that."
- Keep it short and punchy.

Examples:
- "Dogs can recognize over 1,000 human words."
- "Ancient humans walked an average of 20 miles per day."
- "Your brain processes dog barks 3x faster than human speech."

### Type C — Comparison
Present a quick visual or verbal comparison that helps viewers understand the contrast.

Content rules:
- Write a clear side-by-side comparison between two things.
- The comparison should be immediately understandable.
- Use the format: "[Thing A] vs [Thing B]" or a vivid contrast sentence.

Examples:
- "Ancient wolves howled warnings — modern dogs negotiate salaries."
- "1900: humans trained dogs. 2025: dogs train humans."
- "Left: a caveman shouts at clouds. Right: a dog hosts a TED talk."

---

## Critical Rules

IMPORTANT: Break frames contain REAL CONTENT — questions, facts, or comparisons.
Break frames are NEVER just a letter, label, or empty placeholder.
"Type A" means the CONTENT TYPE is a question — it does NOT mean display the letter "A".
"Type B" means the CONTENT TYPE is a fact — it does NOT mean display the letter "B".
"Type C" means the CONTENT TYPE is a comparison — it does NOT mean display the letter "C".

---

## Visual Design
Break Frames should be visually distinct from regular story scenes.
Use bold composition with a clean layout.
The message should be the immediate visual focus.

---

## Story Connection
Every Break Frame must directly relate to the surrounding story.
After the Break Frame, the story should continue smoothly.

---

## Forbidden
Do not display just a letter (A, B, C) as break frame content.
Do not leave break frame content empty or vague.
Do not interrupt important explanations.
Do not introduce unrelated information.
Do not make Break Frames longer than necessary.
